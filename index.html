Daniel Lis

I am an economist in the Office of the Budget at The Regional Municipality of York. I work with the Region's 100-year fiscal model, the basis for its reserve and debt strategy, and I analyze assessment growth and the development charge bylaws that fund $21.2 billion in growth infrastructure to 2051. A lot of that work is about protecting the Region's AAA credit rating over the long run.

Before York, I spent a year at the Office of the New York State Comptroller, which acts as the State's watchdog on New York City's finances. I reviewed the City's quarterly budgets and wrote about City and State revenues, and that research was cited by the New York Times, the Wall Street Journal, Bloomberg, Gothamist and Crain's. Earlier I was a research fellow in the Markets Group at the Federal Reserve Bank of New York, where I studied how U.S. interest rates and the 2022 inflation shock moved through emerging markets.

I also work part-time as Director of Research at HODL Markets, a fintech company, writing about crypto regulation and market structure. I hold an MPA from Columbia University's School of International and Public Affairs. My CV is here.

I am on the job market for economist roles.

Commentary

Written for HODL Markets Insights.

  • The End of Crypto and Security Law August 2026 The SEC's Regulation Crypto Assets gives crypto-native tokens a self-certified safe harbour, while tokenized securities still file on forms built for corporate equity.
  • Are Prediction Markets an Unserious Venture? with Keeyan Ravanshid, August 2026 A two-sided debate on Canadian regulation. I argue for writing the rules now: bans push trading offshore, and regulated exchanges already catch insiders within months.
  • Tether's Audit Won't Fix What's Really Broken June 2026 Tether's first Big Four audit is years late. It also does nothing about Cantor Fitzgerald holding most of Tether's Treasuries while owning equity in the company.
  • Who Owes a Fiduciary Duty in Crypto? June 2026 Follows fiduciary law from Keech v Sandford (1726) to the Lido and bZx cases, and asks who answers for a protocol when its governance fails.
  • $285M Gone a-Drift May 2026 The Drift Protocol exploit read as a governance failure. $285 million left in twelve minutes, and the attacker never touched the smart contracts.

Earlier Commentary

Written at GoQuant in 2022 and 2023. GoQuant no longer hosts them, so they are archived here with the company's permission.

  • Kraken: The Regulation Dilemma GoQuant, February 2023 On Kraken's $30 million SEC settlement over staking. Argues the case showed how little guidance regulators had given crypto firms on how to register.
  • Unstablecoins: Tether and USDC GoQuant, January 2023 Compares Tether's thin cash reserves with Circle's audited USDC reserves. Argues Circle should keep fractional reserves and publish hourly proof of reserves through a service like Chainlink.
  • Gaining the Competitive Edge Through Crypto Regulation: FTX, MICA and You GoQuant, November 2022 Asks whether the EU's MiCA rules would have prevented FTX's collapse. Probably not on reserves alone, but firms that adopt rules like it early can lead the market for 'good-faith crypto.'
  • What the FTX? Regulate Crypto Like It's the 19th Century GoQuant, November 2022 Compares the FTX collapse with North American banking before the Federal Reserve. Argues crypto needs regulation in exchange for a government-backed backstop. Correction: the Federal Reserve was created in 1913, after the Panic of 1907. The piece gives 1911 and 1909.
